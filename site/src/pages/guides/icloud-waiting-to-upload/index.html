iCloud Drive stuck on “Waiting to Upload” on your Mac: what it means and how to fix it safely

The short answer

“Waiting to Upload” means the file is on your Mac but not yet stored in iCloud. Until it uploads, this Mac has the only copy. Usually iCloud catches up on its own. If it hasn't after a few hours:

  1. Check your connection, Apple's System Status page and your iCloud storage.
  2. Copy the waiting files to a folder outside iCloud Drive.
  3. Restart your Mac.
  4. Restart iCloud's sync process with killall bird in Terminal (no sudo).
  5. If a new small file does upload, move a stuck file out of iCloud Drive and back, one file at a time.

Don't delete CloudDocs folders, don't sign out of iCloud or turn off iCloud Drive while files are waiting, and don't kill nsurlsessiond.

What each iCloud status in Finder means

Finder shows a small icon next to items in iCloud Drive. To see them as words, open iCloud Drive in List view (View › as List), then choose View › Show View Options and turn on iCloud Status. These are the statuses in Apple's Mac User Guide:

Why files get stuck

  • iCloud is just busy. After you set up a Mac, turn on Desktop & Documents or add a lot of files, uploading can take hours.
  • Your iCloud storage is full. Finder says Out of Space, and nothing more can upload until you free up space or add storage.
  • No connection, or trouble on Apple's side. Check your internet and Apple's System Status page.
  • A file iCloud won't take. Items over 50 GB are Ineligible. Names ending in .nosync or .tmp are never synced, by design, so they aren't stuck at all. And Eclectic Light found that files with certain lock flags fail to sync on macOS 26.5.
  • Folders with huge numbers of changing files. Developer folders such as node_modules, .git or build output can hold tens of thousands of small files that change constantly, and keep iCloud busy for good.
  • iCloud's sync process has stalled for the whole Mac. The telltale sign: even a brand-new small file won't upload.
  • A macOS bug. Two recent examples: macOS 26.2 stopped packages such as Pages documents and Xcode projects from syncing, fixed in 26.3, and on 26.4.1 developers reported larger uploads hanging (not confirmed by Apple).

Safe fixes, in order

Start at the top and stop as soon as your files upload. Each step is safe on its own.

  1. Check the basics. Make sure you're online, check Apple's System Status page, and look at your iCloud storage in System Settings › your name › iCloud.
  2. Find out whether it's some files or everything. Save a small new file, such as a short text note, in iCloud Drive and watch it for a few minutes. If it uploads, iCloud works and only certain files are stuck. If it doesn't, sync is stalled for the whole Mac: don't move or rename individual files yet. Carry on with the steps below, but skip step 7.
  3. Make a copy of everything that says Waiting to Upload, in a folder outside iCloud Drive, such as an external drive or a new folder in your home folder. If Desktop & Documents sync to iCloud, those two folders are in iCloud too, so don't use them.
  4. Quit apps that have the files open, and stop anything that keeps changing files, such as builds or development servers.
  5. Restart your Mac. A restart is gentler than switching iCloud settings off and on.
  6. Restart iCloud's sync process. Open Terminal and run killall bird, without sudo. bird is the background process that syncs iCloud Drive, and macOS starts it again by itself. Do this once, then give it a few minutes. Don't run it over and over.
  7. Move a stuck file out and back. If step 2 showed that iCloud works, move one stuck file from iCloud Drive to a folder outside it, such as the one you used in step 3, wait for everything else to finish uploading, then move it back. One file at a time. This is the method Howard Oakley recommends. Keep your copy from step 3 until the file shows as uploaded.
  8. Remove the cause. Free up or add iCloud storage, keep items over 50 GB outside iCloud Drive, and move developer folders out of it.
  9. Still stuck after a day? Contact Apple Support (see below).

Don't do this

  • Don't delete the CloudDocs folders. Some guides tell you to delete ~/Library/Application Support/CloudDocs or iCloud's CloudKit caches. Those folders are iCloud's own record of what has synced; deleting them forces a full resync at best. And ~/Library/Mobile Documents/com~apple~CloudDocs, with a similar name, isn't a cache at all: it is your iCloud Drive. Mixing the two up is how people lose files.
  • Don't sign out of iCloud or turn off iCloud Drive while files are waiting to upload. Files that exist only on your Mac are exactly the ones at risk: TidBITS reported files coming back empty (0 KB) after turning iCloud Drive off. First make sure they have uploaded, or that you have your own copy.
  • Don't kill nsurlsessiond. Commands like killall nsurlsessiond, especially with sudo, stop the system's own background transfer service along with yours. Developers on Apple's forums warn against it.
  • Don't turn off Optimize Mac Storage unless your Mac has room for everything. Turning it off makes macOS download every file stored only in iCloud. If those files add up to more than your free space, you can't turn it off safely.
  • Don't run permission-repair tools on your Library folder. One user reported a Mac that could no longer start up after a permissions tool was applied to everything in ~/Library.
  • Don't use sudo for any of this. Nothing in this guide needs administrator rights.

When it's Apple's side

Some problems can't be fixed from your Mac. It's likely on Apple's side when System Status shows an iCloud Drive problem, when new files won't upload from your other devices either, or when nothing changes after a restart and restarting bird. Problems with your Apple Account also need Apple.

Contact Apple Support and tell them which files are stuck, since when, your macOS version and what you've already tried. Don't sign out or reset anything until the files that exist only on your Mac are copied somewhere safe. Deleted files can often be restored from Recently Deleted in iCloud Drive on iCloud.com, where Apple keeps them for 30 days.
