How it works

Four calm steps. You stay in charge of every change.

  1. Scan

    Whydunit reads the sync status of your files in iCloud Drive, plus Desktop and Documents if they sync (app folders such as Pages and Numbers aren't checked yet). It downloads nothing, and checks that sync works by uploading one small test file.

  2. Plain-English diagnosis

    Each problem gets a name, what it means and what to do next: iCloud storage full, a file too big for iCloud, sync stalled for the whole Mac, files that exist only on this Mac, and more.

  3. Verified backup

    Before Retry Upload moves a file, it needs a verified backup of that exact file. Back Up copies it to a Whydunit Backups folder outside iCloud and checks every copy against the original with SHA-256.

  4. Safe retry

    Stuck files are moved out of iCloud Drive and back, one at a time. If sync has stalled for the whole Mac, Whydunit can restart iCloud's sync process, with your OK.

Questions

Will Whydunit delete my files?

No. Whydunit has no delete feature, and an automated check on every code change rejects code that calls the system's permanent-delete functions. A scan only reads sync status. Retry Upload moves a file out of iCloud Drive and straight back, and only after a verified backup of that file exists. The only things Whydunit moves to the Trash are its own test file and folder, and backups you choose to remove.

Does it need Full Disk Access?

Usually not. macOS may ask you to allow access to iCloud Drive, and to Desktop and Documents if they sync to iCloud. You can change this any time in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Files & Folders. If Whydunit isn't listed under Files & Folders, the app will show you how to add it under Full Disk Access instead.

Is my data sent anywhere?

Your file names and contents never leave your Mac. The app goes online for two things only: looking for updates, and the sync check, which uploads one small file of random bytes to your own iCloud Drive and then moves it to the Trash. The privacy policy lists exactly what is sent.

What if I'm offline?

The scan and diagnosis work offline, though iCloud can't upload anything without a connection, and the sync check will tell you so. Whydunit needs no account.

Does it work on Intel Macs?

Yes. Whydunit is a universal app for Apple silicon and Intel Macs running macOS {{minMacOS}} or later.

Can it fix every iCloud problem?

No, and it will tell you so. Some problems are on Apple's side, such as an iCloud outage or an account problem, and nothing on your Mac can fix those. When that looks likely, Whydunit says so and gives you a diagnosis to send to Apple Support.

Is it really free?

Yes. Every feature, on as many Macs as you like, with every update. There are no ads, no in-app purchases, no subscription and no account.

Something isn't working. Where do I get help?

Report it on GitHub Issues, with a copied diagnosis if you can. The support page shows how.

Is it on the Mac App Store?

No. Whydunit needs to restart iCloud's sync process and read sync status across iCloud Drive, which the App Store's sandbox doesn't allow. It is signed with an Apple Developer ID, notarized by Apple, and updates itself.
